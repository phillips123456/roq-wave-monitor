Major volatility shocks have rarely started at the top of a wave. They tend to begin on the way down: about half began one to three weeks after the peak zone ended, a stretch that covers less than a third of trading days. This shows where risk has been concentrated, not a forecast of any single event. The wave left its peak zone on Nov 2. What I'm watching

A fifth day in the trough, and the heaviest selling of this decline. The S&P 500 fell more than 3.5% and the Nasdaq 100 fund more than 5.5%, and volatility reached its most extreme reading of the move. This is the kind of day the defensive mix is built for: the large-cap tilt lost about three quarters as much as a growth tilt. Internal momentum made another new low. Troughs that run this long often have a few more sessions in them, and momentum frequently bottoms after the wave itself has turned. What I'm watching for is the wave and momentum turning up together, with volatility coming back down. The model stays defensively long until that happens.
